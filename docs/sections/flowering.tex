\section{Flowering: the per-flower Markov chain}\label{sec:flowering}
\stagebox{\statuspaused. Only the 2D Python prototype exists.}{\src{prototype-python/automark_bloom/petal_markov.py} (prototype); 3D core not started}

Flowering takes over once the bud structure is fixed. Each growth step depends
probabilistically on the previous state, and each flower draws from its own
seed (\cref{sec:seeds}). Siblings already differ in their buds
(\cref{sec:tiers}); the opening adds its own noise on top.

Andres has put building the 3D flowering stage on hold. This section records
the prototype that exists and the approved design, and will be rewritten when
the code lands.

\subsection{The prototype (2D, discrete)}
\paragraph{State.} Each petal carries three bin indices: length $\lambda$ (14 bins,
0.08 to 0.72 flower radii), width $\omega$ (12 bins, 0.06 to 0.60) and curl
$\gamma$ (13 bins, $-20^\circ$ to $100^\circ$; positive cups inward, negative
bends outward). Every petal starts as a closed bud: shortest, narrowest and
fully curled. The walk runs 45 steps.

\paragraph{Targets.} Each petal has a target bin for each variable, set by its
place in the bud: outer petals end long, wide and flat, inner petals short and
cupped. For a petal whose outer edge sits at relative radius $\pi_1\in[0,1]$,
the target length is $0.22 + 0.48\,\pi_1$ and the target curl is
$80^\circ - 95^\circ\pi_1$; the target width comes from the petal's angular span
in the bud grid.

\paragraph{Moves.} At every step each index $i$ moves by $+1$, $0$ or $-1$.
With $d = i^\star - i$ the (fractional) distance to the target bin and
$\sigma(z) = 1/(1+e^{-z})$,
\[
  \Pr(+1) = g_\uparrow\,\sigma(1.5\,d), \qquad
  \Pr(-1) = g_\downarrow\,\sigma(-1.5\,d), \qquad
  \Pr(0) = 1 - \Pr(+1) - \Pr(-1),
\]
so the walk drifts toward the target but keeps jittering around it. Indices are
clipped to their bins.
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule
Variable & $g_\uparrow$ & $g_\downarrow$ \\
\midrule
length & 0.55 & 0.20 \\
width & $0.50\,e^{-1.5P}$ & 0.20 \\
curl & 0.35 & 0.45 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Packing pressure.} $P$ is a petal's fractional angular overlap with
neighbours within two rings, computed from the current state. Crowding
suppresses widening (the $e^{-1.5P}$ factor) and raises the curl target by
$30^\circ\min(P,1)$, so crowded petals bend instead of spreading. Because $P$
couples petals, the Markov property holds for the joint state of all petals,
not for each petal on its own.

\subsection{The approved 3D design}\label{sec:flowering-design}
\stagebox{\statusplanned}{\src{automark-bloom/species/design-decisions-opening.md} (shared folder)}

\paragraph{Opening is growth plus relaxation.} Real opening is irreversible
cell expansion following a mostly fixed program: an S-shaped growth curve that
starts with the outer layers. Variation sits on top of that program, in each
petal's start time and growth rate, and contact with neighbours shapes the
result. The model copies that split:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item the \emph{growth program} (species tier, \cref{sec:tiers}) sets how fast
  each quantity should grow at each age;
\item the \emph{Markov chain} supplies only the noise, through a slowly
  drifting rate factor per petal;
\item the bud stage's \emph{energy relaxation} (\cref{sec:packing}) turns the
  grown quantities into geometry.
\end{itemize}
Curl, reflex and ruffles come out of differential growth plus contact. They
are not an independent curl state as in the prototype.

\paragraph{State.} The chain is Markov over the whole flower: the state at a
step holds everything the next step needs. Per petal $j$ that is its sheet
geometry $X_j$, its accumulated growth quantities $\vect q_j$, a rate factor
$\rho_j$ and its age $a_j$. The growth quantities are
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
Quantity & Shape it produces \\
\midrule
length, width & size \\
edge growth versus midline & flare and ruffling \\
one-face growth at the base & reflex (folding back) \\
face-versus-face growth across the blade & cupping \\
left versus right edge growth (optional) & twist, lopsided curl \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Thickness thins as area grows and stays under the cap $\tmax$.

\paragraph{One step.} Each step
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item drifts each rate factor by mean-reverting noise, for example
  $\rho_j \leftarrow \bar\rho + \kappa(\rho_j - \bar\rho) + s\,\xi_j$ with
  $\xi_j\sim\Norm(0,1)$, so a petal that starts fast tends to stay fast;
\item adds growth increments $\Delta\vect q_j = \rho_j\, G(a_j;\ \text{latents}_j)$
  from the growth program $G$, never negative for size;
\item maps the new growth quantities to the energy's targets (rest lengths and
  preferred curvature) and relaxes the energy, so petals keep their thickness
  and never pass through each other;
\item writes a frame of the flower JSON with the same topology
  (\cref{sec:output}).
\end{enumerate}
Each petal has its own noise on every quantity, so petals differ in rate and in
how they curl, as well as through their layer and their contacts.

\paragraph{Inputs: the bud's latent quantities.} The chain starts from each
petal's packed sheet and its latent quantities (\cref{sec:tiers}): growth
potentials, shape tendencies, timing and fixed type.

\subsection{Latent channels}\label{sec:channels}
\stagebox{\statusbuilt{} for the channel store and the bud's channels; the opening stepper is \statusplanned}{\src{core/src/channels.rs}, \src{core/src/latents.rs}}

New latent quantities, such as colour gradients for the logistic-map stage
(\cref{sec:colour}), must be addable without modifying the opening machinery.
Every latent quantity is therefore a generic \emph{channel}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item A channel is declared once with a name, a scope (point, petal or
  flower), a number of dimensions, an optional range and a kind: \emph{fixed}
  (never changes), \emph{target} (a goal a growth quantity moves toward) or
  \emph{state} (updated by the stage that owns it). Categorical channels also
  carry value labels. Consumers look channels up by name and the machinery
  never knows what one means.
\item Each channel draws from its own random sub-stream keyed by a hash of its
  name (\cref{sec:seeds}), so adding a channel never shifts the draws of the
  others. A test checks this.
\item The flower JSON carries the declarations and the values generically
  (\cref{sec:output}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What the bud hands over.} With $\delta_j\in[0,1]$ the petal's depth
in the bud (0 innermost layer, 1 outermost), $n$ the plant's
\texttt{latent\_noise}, $\xi\sim\Norm(0,1)$ from the channel's own stream, and
``noisy'' meaning $x(1 + n\,\xi)$:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l l l X@{}}
\toprule
Channel & Scope & Kind & Value \\
\midrule
\texttt{target\_length} & petal & target & noisy size potential $\times$ (0.5 + fraction of shell rows its patch reached) \\
\texttt{target\_width} & petal & target & noisy size potential $\times$ (0.3 + widest row in cells / 24) \\
\texttt{edge\_growth} & petal & target & noisy \texttt{edge\_boost} \\
\texttt{cup} & petal & target & noisy blend from \texttt{cup\_inner} to \texttt{cup\_outer} by depth, in $[0,1]$ \\
\texttt{base\_fold} & petal & target & noisy blend from \texttt{fold\_inner} to \texttt{fold\_outer} by depth, in $[0,1]$ \\
\texttt{onset} & petal & fixed & $\texttt{onset\_spread}\,(1-\delta_j) + 0.05\,\xi$, in $[0,1]$; outer petals open first \\
\texttt{bud\_growth} & petal & state & mean accumulated growth $S$ of the petal's nodes \\
\texttt{petal\_type} & petal & fixed & flat, cupped or rolled, by the species weights \\
\texttt{twist} & petal & fixed & $+1$ (clockwise) or $-1$ \\
\texttt{vigour} & flower & fixed & the flower's vigour $\nu$ \\
\texttt{sepal\_radius} & flower & state & envelope radius $R$ at the end of the bud stage \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\paragraph{How the opening will use them (design).} Each channel kind gets an
update rule from a small set (monotone growth toward a target, mean-reverting
drift, fixed), and the stepper loops over channels without knowing what they
mean. Channels the geometry uses feed the relaxation step through one mapping
layer; a channel the geometry ignores, such as colour, simply evolves and is
exported. Adding a channel means declaring it and, if needed, its consumer. The
stepper, the random streams and the export format stay unchanged.

\paragraph{Ideas still to fold in} from the original design notes: phyllotactic
spiral angle and petals growing larger outward.
