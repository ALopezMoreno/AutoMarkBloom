\section{Flowering: the per-flower Markov chain}\label{sec:flowering}
\stagebox{\statusbuilt}{\src{core/src/bloom/mod.rs}, \src{core/src/bloom/program.rs}, \src{core/src/bloom/channels.rs}, \src{core/src/bloom/consume.rs}, \src{core/src/bloom/relax.rs}, \src{core/src/bloom/contact.rs}}

Flowering opens the packed bud. It takes each petal's packed sheet and the
latent channels the bud hands over (\cref{sec:channels}), and steps the whole
flower through time as a Markov chain: each step depends only on the state at
the start of the step. Each flower draws from its own seed, so siblings,
which already differ in their buds (\cref{sec:tiers}), open differently too.

\subsection{How it is split}
Real opening is irreversible cell expansion following a mostly fixed program:
an S-shaped growth curve that starts with the outer layers. Variation sits on
top of that program, in each petal's start time and growth rate, and contact
with neighbours shapes the result. The model copies that split:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item a \emph{channel program} (\cref{sec:opening-program}) says how each
  quantity moves toward its goal over time; the only noise is a slowly drifting
  rate per petal;
\item a \emph{pose} (\cref{sec:opening-pose}) turns the channels into where
  each petal would like to be: its centre line, width, cup, splay and
  crumples, plus rest lengths and thickness;
\item a \emph{relaxation} (\cref{sec:opening-relax}) pulls the petals toward
  their poses while keeping them from passing through each other or the core.
\end{itemize}
Curl, reflex, cup and splay are therefore prescribed shapes that bending and a
weak pose spring pull toward, not the product of differential growth. Only the
margin ruffling (edge growth and left-right asymmetry) comes from differential
rest lengths.

\paragraph{Time.} The opening runs from $t=0$ (the bud) to $t=1$ in
$N = 24$ steps of $\Delta t = 1/24$ by default. Step $k$ advances every channel
from $t_k = k/N$ to $t_{k+1}$, builds every petal's pose, relaxes, and records a
frame at $t_{k+1}$. Steps can be split into substeps, with a frame written
after each full step.

\paragraph{Random stream.} The opening's seed is
$\mathrm{derive}(s_i, \texttt{0x0B100000})$ from the flower seed $s_i$. Every
channel then gets its own generator keyed by the FNV-1a hash of its name, and
within a channel each petal has its own PCG32 stream, so adding a channel
never changes the draws of any other (a test checks this).

\subsection{Latent channels}\label{sec:channels}
\stagebox{\statusbuilt}{\src{core/src/channels.rs}, \src{core/src/latents.rs}, \src{core/src/bloom/channels.rs}}

New latent quantities, such as colour gradients for the logistic-map stage
(\cref{sec:colour}), must be addable without modifying the opening machinery.
Every latent quantity is therefore a generic \emph{channel}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item A channel is declared once with a name, a scope (flower, petal or
  point), a number of dimensions (1 or 3), an optional range, an update rule
  and a default value. Categorical channels also carry value labels. Consumers
  look channels up by name and the machinery never knows what one means.
\item A rule's parameters are either constants or the names of other
  channels. A channel may read channels of its own or a coarser scope (a petal
  channel can read a flower channel, not a point channel). Unknown names,
  duplicate names and mismatched dimensions are rejected when the program is
  built.
\item The flower JSON carries the declarations and the values generically
  (\cref{sec:output}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Rules.} With $x$ the value at $t_k$ and every parameter read from the
state at $t_k$ (so the result does not depend on the order of channels):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{fixed} and \emph{state}: unchanged by the stepper (a state
  channel is written by the stage that owns it);
\item \emph{drift}: mean-reverting noise (Euler--Maruyama),
  \[
    x \leftarrow x + r\,(\mu - x)\,\Delta t + \sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}\;\xi, \qquad \xi\sim\Norm(0,1);
  \]
\item \emph{growth}: moves toward a goal $g = \text{gain}\cdot\text{target} +
  \text{bias}$ inside a window that opens at the channel's onset and lasts
  $D$. With $S(t) = \operatorname{smoothstep}\bigl(\clamp((t - \text{onset})/D, 0, 1)\bigr)$
  and $\operatorname{smoothstep}(y) = y^2(3-2y)$,
  \[
    x \leftarrow x + (g - x)\,\min\Bigl(1,\; \text{rate}\,\frac{S(t_{k+1}) - S(t_k)}{1 - S(t_k)}\Bigr),
  \]
  and nothing happens outside the window. With rate 1 and a constant goal, $x$
  lands exactly on the goal when the window closes; slower petals stop short
  and faster ones arrive early. A \emph{monotone} growth never moves
  backwards;
\item \emph{derived}: $x = \text{gain}\cdot\operatorname{sgn}(y)\,|y|^p + \text{bias}$
  of another channel $y$, evaluated from the start.
\end{itemize}
Only drift draws random numbers.

\paragraph{What the bud hands over.} The bud declares these channels with the
kinds \emph{target}, \emph{fixed} and \emph{state}; the opening program gives
each its rule. With $\delta_j\in[0,1]$ the petal's depth
in the bud (0 innermost layer, 1 outermost), $n$ the plant's
\texttt{latent\_noise}, $\xi\sim\Norm(0,1)$ from the channel's own stream, and
``noisy'' meaning $x(1 + n\,\xi)$:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l l l X@{}}
\toprule
Channel & Scope & Kind & Value \\
\midrule
\texttt{target\_length} & petal & target & noisy size potential $\times$ (0.5 + fraction of shell rows its patch reached) \\
\texttt{target\_width} & petal & target & noisy size potential $\times$ (0.3 + widest row in cells / 24) \\
\texttt{edge\_growth} & petal & target & noisy \texttt{edge\_boost} \\
\texttt{cup} & petal & target & noisy blend from \texttt{cup\_inner} to \texttt{cup\_outer} by depth, in $[0,1]$ \\
\texttt{base\_fold} & petal & target & noisy blend from \texttt{fold\_inner} to \texttt{fold\_outer} by depth, in $[0,1]$ \\
\texttt{onset} & petal & fixed & $\texttt{onset\_spread}\,(1-\delta_j) + 0.05\,\xi$, in $[0,1]$; outer petals open first \\
\texttt{bud\_growth} & petal & state & mean accumulated growth $S$ of the petal's nodes \\
\texttt{petal\_type} & petal & fixed & flat, cupped or rolled, by the species weights \\
\texttt{twist} & petal & fixed & $+1$ (clockwise) or $-1$ \\
\texttt{vigour} & flower & fixed & the flower's vigour $\nu$ \\
\texttt{sepal\_radius} & flower & state & envelope radius $R$ at the end of the bud stage \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

The opening adds one input of its own, \texttt{layer\_depth}: the petal's
layer divided by the outermost layer of the bud (0 innermost, 1 outermost).
Bud channels that no rule mentions pass through unchanged and are exported.

\subsection{The peony opening program}\label{sec:opening-program}
The program is data: a list of channel declarations. In the default one,
$\delta$ is the layer depth and every growth window lasts $D = 0.55$ from the
petal's \texttt{onset}, at the petal's \texttt{rate}:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l l X@{}}
\toprule
Channel & Rule & Meaning \\
\midrule
\texttt{rate} & drift to \texttt{vigour}, $r=3$, $\sigma=0.35$ & per-petal speed, from 1; a fast petal tends to stay fast \\
\texttt{length} & growth to \texttt{target\_length}, monotone & length factor, from 1 \\
\texttt{width} & growth to $1.6\,$\texttt{target\_width}, monotone & width factor, from 1 \\
\texttt{edge} & growth to $0.25\,$\texttt{edge\_growth}, monotone & extra margin length (flare, ruffles), from 0 \\
\texttt{cupping} & growth to \texttt{cup} & cupping across the blade, from 0 \\
\texttt{reflex} & growth to $0.6\,$\texttt{base\_fold} & outward fold at the base (rad), from 0 \\
\texttt{asymmetry} & growth to $0.08\,$\texttt{twist} & left-right margin difference, from 0 \\
\texttt{splay\_angle} & derived, $0.35 + 1.30\,\delta^3$ & where the petal ends up pointing (rad from vertical) \\
\texttt{splay} & growth to 1, monotone & how far the petal has turned toward its splay angle, from 0 \\
\texttt{curl\_keep} & derived, $0.8 - 0.65\,\delta^3$ & how much of its bud curl a petal keeps \\
\texttt{memory} & growth to $0.6\,$\texttt{cup} $+$ \texttt{curl\_keep} & bud curl along the petal, from 1 \\
\texttt{wrap} & growth to $0.5\,$\texttt{cup} $+$ \texttt{curl\_keep} & bud wrap round the axis, from 1 \\
\texttt{ruffle} & growth to 0.6 & how much of the bud's crumpling stays, from 1 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
The rate's stationary spread is $0.35/\sqrt{2\cdot3} \approx 0.14$. The splay
and curl profiles come from the species' opening shape (angle 0.35 to 1.65 rad,
curl kept 0.8 to 0.15, exponent 3), so outer petals open wide and flatten while
the innermost stay upright and incurved over the centre, as in a peony. Bud
channels supply the targets; the program's own defaults (length 1.5, width
1.3, cup 0.5, fold 0.3, edge 1, twist 0) apply only when a bud lacks them.

\paragraph{Timing.} The bud sets \texttt{onset} from 0 for the outermost layer
up to \texttt{onset\_spread} (0.4 to 0.8) for the innermost. A window that
starts after $t = 0.45$ is still open at $t = 1$, so in the default program the
innermost petals are still opening on the last frame (at onset 0.8 and rate 1
they are about 30\% of the way there), much as a peony looks just after it
opens.

\subsection{From channels to a pose}\label{sec:opening-pose}
For each petal, on its $11\times19$ control grid, the pose is built from the
petal's laid-out sheet (where it sat on its shell before packing) and its
packed sheet:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Centre line.} The laid sheet's centre column is a curve in its
  meridian plane. It is redrawn with each turning angle scaled by
  \texttt{memory}, an extra outward turn of \texttt{reflex} radians spread over
  a hinge near the base, $h(f) = \exp(-((f-0.12)/0.1)^2)$, and segments
  stretched by the length factor (ramped in over the first 15\% of the petal).
\item \textbf{Splay.} The angle of the chord from base to tip is moved a
  fraction \texttt{splay} of the way to \texttt{splay\_angle}, by adding to the
  base fold and redrawing the curve (four corrections).
\item \textbf{Rows.} Each row is widened by the width factor (ramped in over
  the first 25\%), but at most until it spans half a turn round the axis; a row
  already wider in the bud keeps its width. Without that cap, a petal that
  wraps far round a small core spirals through its neighbours as it widens.
  The row is blended between lying on its circle (\texttt{wrap}) and lying
  flat on its tangent, then dipped by $\tfrac12 k\,\text{arc}^2$ with a
  curvature $k$ set by \texttt{cupping}.
\item \textbf{Crumples.} The bud's crumpling (the packed sheet's offset from
  the laid one, in the petal's own frame of normal, across and along) is added
  back scaled by \texttt{ruffle}. Which face is outward is decided once on the
  laid sheet: deciding it again on a nearly horizontal, splayed petal would
  flip its crumples to the other face.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Rest lengths and thickness.} Springs take their length in the pose,
times a margin factor
\[
  e = \bigl(1 + \text{edge}\,(2f_u-1)^2 f\bigr)\bigl(1 + \text{asym}\,(2f_u-1)\, f\bigr)
\]
on springs along the petal ($\sqrt{(1+e^2)/2}$ on diagonals), where $f_u$ runs
across the petal, $f$ from base to tip, and asym is \texttt{asymmetry}. So the margins want to be longer than the midline and
ruffle. Thickness thins with area, $t = t_0\,\max(A, 1)^{-0.6}$, where $A$ is
the local product of length, width and margin factors.

With all channels at their neutral values the pose is the packed bud exactly
(a test checks this to $10^{-9}$).

\subsection{Relaxation}\label{sec:opening-relax}
Each step relaxes the control points toward the pose by minimising
\[
  E = E_{\mathrm{spring}} + E_{\mathrm{bend}} + E_{\mathrm{pose}} + E_{\mathrm{contact}} + E_{\mathrm{core}}:
\]
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item springs along, across and diagonally in each cell, stiffness 40 (10 on
  diagonals), at the pose's rest lengths;
\item bending on every three consecutive points along each direction,
  $\tfrac12\cdot20\,(t_0/t_{\mathrm{cap}})^3\,\lVert\vect r\rVert^2$, with
  $\vect r$ the second difference of the displacement from the pose, so the
  pose's own curvature costs nothing;
\item a weak pose spring $\tfrac12\cdot2\,\lVert\x - \vect p\rVert^2$;
\item contact between petals, $\tfrac12\cdot1500\,[d_{\min}-d]_+^2$, evaluated on
  $2\times2$ samples per grid cell (777 per petal) against the other petals'
  triangles, each sample's force spread back to its four control points
  bilinearly so the gradient stays exact;
\item the core, $\tfrac12\cdot400\,[R_0 + t/2 - \rt(\x)]_+^2$.
\end{itemize}
There is no sepal envelope and no layer band any more; the base row stays
pinned. Contact is a soft penalty, and the worst overlap is reported per frame.

Contact candidates are found as in the bud (\cref{sec:packing}), with a skin
of one thickness cap: a flat grid of samples, a plane test before the exact
closest-point test, distance bounds that skip pairs which cannot touch, and a
rebuild only for petals that moved more than a quarter of the skin, merged with
the kept pairs of petals that did not.

FIRE runs 20 iterations per step ($\Delta t_{\max} = 0.06$, steps clipped to a
quarter of the thickness cap, without the bud's per-node masses). Each step
starts from the previous positions shifted by how much the pose moved, and
carries FIRE's velocity and step size over from the previous step, so the
opening is one continuous relaxation of 480 iterations.

\subsection{Output}
The opening returns one frame per step at $t = 1/24, \dots, 1$, each with
every petal's control grid, thickness and channel values, its energy and its
worst overlap. Export appends them to the bud's flower JSON as frames with
\texttt{stage: "open"} (\cref{sec:output}). The command-line tool runs it with
\texttt{--open N}; the browser build does not run the opening, and the viewer
plays a precomputed sample.

\subsection{The 2D prototype}
The first opening model, in \src{prototype-python/automark_bloom/petal_markov.py},
ran on the flat 2D bud. It is kept for reference; the 3D stage above replaces
it.

\paragraph{State.} Each petal carries three bin indices: length $\lambda$ (14 bins,
0.08 to 0.72 flower radii), width $\omega$ (12 bins, 0.06 to 0.60) and curl
$\gamma$ (13 bins, $-20^\circ$ to $100^\circ$; positive cups inward, negative
bends outward). Every petal starts as a closed bud: shortest, narrowest and
fully curled. The walk runs 45 steps.

\paragraph{Targets.} Each petal has a target bin for each variable, set by its
place in the bud: outer petals end long, wide and flat, inner petals short and
cupped. For a petal whose outer edge sits at relative radius $\pi_1\in[0,1]$,
the target length is $0.22 + 0.48\,\pi_1$ and the target curl is
$80^\circ - 95^\circ\pi_1$; the target width comes from the petal's angular span
in the bud grid.

\paragraph{Moves.} At every step each index $i$ moves by $+1$, $0$ or $-1$.
With $d = i^\star - i$ the (fractional) distance to the target bin and
$\sigma(z) = 1/(1+e^{-z})$,
\[
  \Pr(+1) = g_\uparrow\,\sigma(1.5\,d), \qquad
  \Pr(-1) = g_\downarrow\,\sigma(-1.5\,d), \qquad
  \Pr(0) = 1 - \Pr(+1) - \Pr(-1),
\]
so the walk drifts toward the target but keeps jittering around it. Indices are
clipped to their bins.
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule
Variable & $g_\uparrow$ & $g_\downarrow$ \\
\midrule
length & 0.55 & 0.20 \\
width & $0.50\,e^{-1.5P}$ & 0.20 \\
curl & 0.35 & 0.45 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Packing pressure.} $P$ is a petal's fractional angular overlap with
neighbours within two rings, computed from the current state. Crowding
suppresses widening (the $e^{-1.5P}$ factor) and raises the curl target by
$30^\circ\min(P,1)$, so crowded petals bend instead of spreading. Because $P$
couples petals, the Markov property holds for the joint state of all petals,
not for each petal on its own.
