\subsection{The random number generator}
All randomness comes from one fully specified generator, so a seed means the
same random stream in every build, engine and language port. No library or
operating-system generator is used.

\paragraph{SplitMix64} mixes a 64-bit integer $z$ (all arithmetic modulo $2^{64}$,
$\oplus$ is XOR, $\gg$ is a logical shift):
\begin{align*}
z &\leftarrow z + \texttt{0x9E3779B97F4A7C15},\\
z &\leftarrow (z \oplus (z \gg 30)) \cdot \texttt{0xBF58476D1CE4E5B9},\\
z &\leftarrow (z \oplus (z \gg 27)) \cdot \texttt{0x94D049BB133111EB},\\
\mathrm{sm}(z) &= z \oplus (z \gg 31).
\end{align*}

\paragraph{Sub-streams.} Each part of the model draws from its own stream, so
changing how many numbers one part consumes never shifts another part's
sequence. For a seed $s$ and stream key $k$,
\[
  \mathrm{derive}(s,k) = \mathrm{sm}\bigl(s \oplus \mathrm{sm}(k + \texttt{0x5EED})\bigr),
\]
and the stream's generator is PCG32 seeded with $\mathrm{derive}(s,k)$ and
increment $2k+1$.

Plant traits come from stream 0 of the plant seed $s$. Flower $i$ of the plant
has its own seed $s_i = \mathrm{derive}(s, 1000 + i)$, and everything that
flower grows draws from sub-streams of $s_i$:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Seed & Key $k$ & Used by \\
\midrule
plant $s$ & 0 & plant trait draws (\cref{sec:traits}) \\
flower $s_i$ & 1 & shell automaton (\cref{sec:ca}) \\
flower $s_i$ & 2 & packing symmetry breaking (\cref{sec:packing}) \\
flower $s_i$ & 3 & growth automaton (\cref{sec:growth}) \\
flower $s_i$ & 4 & flower traits: vigour, layer jitter (\cref{sec:flowertraits}) \\
flower $s_i$ & 5 & seed for the latent channels (\cref{sec:channels}) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Each latent channel then gets its own generator, keyed by a 64-bit FNV-1a hash
$h$ of its name: PCG32 seeded with $\mathrm{derive}(c, h)$ and increment
$2h+1$, where $c = \mathrm{derive}(s_i, 5)$. Adding a channel therefore never
changes the draws of any other channel.

\paragraph{PCG32} (XSH-RR variant) has a 64-bit state $S$ and odd increment $c$:
\[
  S' = 6364136223846793005\,S + c, \qquad
  \text{out} = \mathrm{rotr}_{32}\Bigl(\bigl((S \gg 18) \oplus S\bigr) \gg 27,\; S \gg 59\Bigr).
\]
Derived draws:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item a uniform $U\in[0,1)$ with 53 random bits, built from two outputs
  $a,b$ as $\bigl(2^{26}(a\gg5) + (b\gg6)\bigr)/2^{53}$;
\item $\Unif[\ell,h] = \ell + (h-\ell)U$;
\item a uniform integer in $[\ell,h]$ as $\min(\ell + \lfloor U(h-\ell+1)\rfloor, h)$;
\item a standard normal by Box--Muller, $\sqrt{-2\ln U_1}\cos(2\pi U_2)$;
\item Fisher--Yates shuffles using the integer draw.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Species and plant traits}\label{sec:traits}
A \emph{species} is a set of ranges. A \emph{plant} is one value drawn
uniformly from each range, in a fixed order, from stream 0. The result is an
explicit list of named numbers (\texttt{PlantTraits}), so that inheritance can
later mutate and cross named traits rather than an opaque seed. All
species-specific numbers live in the species; nothing peony-specific is
hard-coded anywhere else. Peony is the built-in preset; other species are JSON
files with the same fields.

The integer traits (layers $L$ and growth stages $G$) are drawn as
$\lfloor\Unif[n_0, n_1+1]\rfloor$, capped at $n_1$ and at least 1, so every
integer in the range is equally likely.

\subsection{Flower traits}\label{sec:flowertraits}
Some species rules apply per flower rather than per plant. From stream 4 of its
own seed, flower $i$ draws
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item a \emph{vigour} $\nu \sim \Unif[0.85, 1.1]$ for peony (main bud versus
  side bud, conditions);
\item a \emph{layer jitter}: with probability 0.4 the flower has one layer
  fewer or one more than the plant, each equally likely (at least one layer).
\end{itemize}
The flower then grows with the plant's traits except that $L$ is its own
jittered layer count and both \texttt{growth\_rate} and \texttt{size\_potential}
are multiplied by $\nu$. The species also fixes the relative weights of petal
types (flat 0.3, cupped 0.6, rolled 0.1 for peony) and the chance a petal
twists clockwise (0.5), which are drawn per petal as channels.
