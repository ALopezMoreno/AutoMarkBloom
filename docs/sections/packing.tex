\section{Bud development: growth, packing and swelling}\label{sec:packing}
\stagebox{\statusbuilt}{\src{core/src/pack.rs}, \src{core/src/growth.rs}, \src{core/src/energy.rs}}

The sheets laid out by \cref{sec:sheets} fit their patches exactly. The bud
then develops over $G$ growth stages (10 to 14 for peony). In each stage a
stochastic automaton on every petal decides which parts of it grow, the
petals' preferred sizes follow that growth, and all petals settle together by
minimising one smooth energy in which the sepal envelope can give way. Uneven
growth forces petals to ruffle and crumple, crowding slows growth where petals
press hardest, and the pressure that remains swells the bud.

\subsection{Unknowns}
The unknowns are the positions $\x_i\in\R^3$ of all control points of all
petals, $X = (\x_1,\dots,\x_n)$, plus the sepal envelope's scaled radius $R$.
Point $i$ belongs to petal $p(i)$ on layer $k(i)$ and has a fixed thickness
$t_i$. The base row ($v=0$) of every petal is pinned to the receptacle and
never moves.

\subsection{The energy}
\[
  E(X, R) = E_{\mathrm{spring}} + E_{\mathrm{bend}} + E_{\mathrm{band}} + E_{\mathrm{env}} + E_{\mathrm{sepal}} + E_{\mathrm{contact}}.
\]
Every term is a spring or a squared hinge $\tfrac12 k\,[\,\cdot\,]_+^2$, so $E$ is
continuously differentiable ($C^1$) and has an exact gradient with respect to
both $X$ and $R$.

\paragraph{Springs (stretch, shear, bending).} Each petal's control grid is
joined by springs $(a,b)$ with stiffness $k_{ab}$ and rest length $\ell_{ab}$:
\[
  E_{\mathrm{spring}} = \sum_{(a,b)} \tfrac12 k_{ab}\bigl(\lVert\x_a - \x_b\rVert - \ell_{ab}\bigr)^2,
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial}{\partial \x_a} = k_{ab}\,\frac{\lVert\x_a - \x_b\rVert - \ell_{ab}}{\lVert\x_a - \x_b\rVert}\,(\x_a - \x_b).
\]

\end{center}
Bending stiffness of a plate grows with the cube of its thickness, so the thin
edges crumple more easily than the thick base. Here $t_a$ is the thickness at
the spring's first point and $k_b$ is the plant's \texttt{petal\_stiffness}
(60 to 120 for peony): stiffer petals fold in broader waves and push the
sepals out more.

\paragraph{Zigzag-resistant bending.} A skip spring between points two apart
cannot feel a single point popping in and out between them, so on its own the
sheet crinkles at the scale of the grid. A second bending term acts on every
three consecutive points $a,b,c$ along $u$ and along $v$, through their second
difference $\vect d = \x_a - 2\x_b + \x_c$:
\[
  E_{\mathrm{bend}} = \sum_{(a,b,c)} \tfrac12 k_{abc}\bigl(\lVert\vect d\rVert - r_{abc}\bigr)^2,
  \qquad k_{abc} = \zeta\,k_b\,(t_a/\tmax)^3,
\]
where $\zeta$ is the plant's \texttt{zigzag\_bending} (3 to 5 for peony) and the
rest value $r^0_{abc}$ is the laid-out second difference, so the shell's own
curvature costs nothing. With $c = k_{abc}(\lVert\vect d\rVert - r_{abc})/\lVert\vect d\rVert$
the gradient is $c\,\vect d$ on $a$ and $c$, and $-2c\,\vect d$ on $b$. Setting
$\zeta = 0$ removes the term and brings the grid-scale crinkle back.

Every spring starts with its laid-out length $\ell^0_{ab}$ as its rest length.
After each growth stage the rest length follows the growth of its two ends
along the spring's own direction,
\[
  \ell_{ab} = \ell^0_{ab}\,\tfrac12\bigl(G_a + G_b\bigr),
  \qquad
  G = \begin{cases} A^\perp & \text{springs along } u \text{ (across the petal)},\\
  A^\parallel & \text{springs along } v \text{ (base to tip)},\\
  S = \sqrt{A^\parallel A^\perp} & \text{diagonal springs,}\end{cases}
\]
where $A^\parallel_i$ and $A^\perp_i$ are node $i$'s accumulated growth along
and across the petal (\cref{sec:growth}). A bend's rest value is
$r^0_{abc}$ times the middle node's $A^\perp$ or $A^\parallel$, by its
direction. Where
growth is uneven, neighbouring springs disagree about how long the petal
should be, and the sheet can only satisfy them by buckling out of its surface.

\paragraph{Loose layer band.} Each point is pulled loosely toward its own
layer, but may stray from it, so petals can fold over each other and between
layers. The layers keep their relative place between the stamens and the
sepals as the bud swells: layer $k$ sits at
\[
  a_k(R) = R_0 + \lambda_k\,(R - R_0), \qquad \lambda_k = \frac{a_k - R_0}{R_{\mathrm{env}} - R_0},
\]
where $a_k$ and $R_{\mathrm{env}}$ are the starting values from
\cref{sec:shells}. With half-width $h_i = \max\bigl(0, (f w - t_i)/2\bigr)$,
where $f$ is the plant's \texttt{layer\_freedom} (1.5 to 3 slots for peony),
and excess $\epsilon_i = \lvert\rt(\x_i) - a_{k(i)}(R)\rvert - h_i$,
\[
  E_{\mathrm{band}} = \sum_i \tfrac12 k_{\mathrm{band}}\,[\epsilon_i]_+^2,
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial}{\partial \x_i} = k_{\mathrm{band}}[\epsilon_i]_+\,\sigma_i\,\nabla\rt(\x_i),
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial}{\partial R} = -\sum_i k_{\mathrm{band}}[\epsilon_i]_+\,\sigma_i\,\lambda_{k(i)},
\]
with $\sigma_i = \operatorname{sign}\bigl(\rt(\x_i) - a_{k(i)}(R)\bigr)$.

\paragraph{Envelope and core.} Points stay inside the sepals and outside the
stamens, each allowing for half its thickness:
\[
  E_{\mathrm{env}} = \sum_i \tfrac12 k_{\mathrm{env}}\Bigl(\bigl[\rt(\x_i) - (R - \tfrac{t_i}{2})\bigr]_+^2 + \bigl[(R_0 + \tfrac{t_i}{2}) - \rt(\x_i)\bigr]_+^2\Bigr).
\]
A point pressing on the envelope pushes $R$ outward with force
$k_{\mathrm{env}}[\rt(\x_i) - R + t_i/2]_+$.

\paragraph{Sepals.} The envelope resists growing past its rest radius
$R_{\mathrm{rest}}$ with the plant's sepal stiffness $k_s$ (\texttt{sepal\_\allowbreak stiffness}):
\[
  E_{\mathrm{sepal}} = \tfrac12 k_s\,(R - R_{\mathrm{rest}})^2 .
\]
The rest radius itself grows a little over development (\cref{sec:stages}).
Soft sepals (small $k_s$) let the bud swell like a marshmallow; stiff ones keep
it tight and force more crumpling inside.

\paragraph{Contact.} Two petals must keep their mid-surfaces at least their
combined half-thickness apart. For a point $\x_i$ and a triangle
$T = (a,b,c)$ of another petal's control mesh (each grid cell is split into two
triangles), let $\vect q = \lambda_a\x_a + \lambda_b\x_b + \lambda_c\x_c$ be the
closest point of $T$ to $\x_i$ (barycentric weights $\lambda$, computed by
Ericson's region test), $d = \lVert\x_i - \vect q\rVert$, and
\[
  d_{\min} = \tfrac12\Bigl(t_i + \tfrac13(t_a + t_b + t_c)\Bigr).
\]
Then
\[
  E_{\mathrm{contact}} = \sum_{(i,T)} \tfrac12 k_{\mathrm{contact}}\,[d_{\min} - d]_+^2 .
\]
Using point-to-triangle distance rather than point-to-point means a point cannot
slip through the gap between two control points of a neighbour. Every point is
tested against triangles of every other petal, so each pair of petals is
checked in both directions.

The gradient follows from the envelope theorem: because $\vect q$ minimises the
distance over $T$, moving $\vect q$ within the triangle does not change $d$ to
first order, so the weights $\lambda$ can be held fixed. With
$\vect n = \x_i - \vect q$ and $\kappa = k_{\mathrm{contact}}[d_{\min}-d]_+/d$,
\[
  \frac{\partial E}{\partial \x_i} = -\kappa\,\vect n,
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial E}{\partial \x_m} = \kappa\,\lambda_m\,\vect n \quad (m\in\{a,b,c\}).
\]
Using the triangle's mean thickness, instead of the thickness at $\vect q$,
keeps $d_{\min}$ independent of $X$, so no extra terms appear. The gradient is
exact wherever the closest point is unique.

\paragraph{Checked by test.} A test squeezes a bud so that the band, envelope
and contact terms are all active, with the envelope below its rest radius, and
compares the analytic gradient with central finite differences ($h=10^{-6}$)
on 60 point coordinates and on $R$.

\subsection{The growth automaton}\label{sec:growth}
Each petal carries a second stochastic automaton on its own $\NU\times\NV$
node grid, drawing from stream 3 of the flower's seed (one generator shared by
all petals, in petal order). Every node $i$ carries accumulated growth along
the petal $A^\parallel_i$ and across it $A^\perp_i$ (both starting at 1, with
$S_i = \sqrt{A^\parallel_i A^\perp_i}$), a persistent noise value $z_i$, and an
active or quiet flag for each stage. The base row never grows.

\paragraph{Correlated, persistent noise.} Each stage every petal draws a fresh
field $\varepsilon$ of $\NU\NV$ standard normals, smooths it with
$\round(c_g^2)$ passes of the neighbour-averaging below, and rescales
it to zero mean and unit variance over the rows $v\ge1$. Here $c_g$ is the
plant's growth correlation (\texttt{growth\_\allowbreak correlation}, 1 to 2
for peony, so 1 to 4 passes). The node's noise then evolves as
\[
  z_i \leftarrow \rho\,z_i + \sqrt{1-\rho^2}\;\varepsilon_i
\]
(on the first stage $z = \varepsilon$), with $\rho$ the
\texttt{growth\_persistence} (0.5 to 0.8). This keeps $z$ at unit variance while
making it correlated in space over $c_g$ nodes and in time over a few stages,
so growing patches are smooth and tend to keep growing.

\paragraph{Activity.} In each stage a node with $v\ge1$ is active when
\[
  \Phi(z_i) < p_i, \qquad
  p_i = a_0 + a_1\,\frac{n^{\mathrm{on}}_i}{n_i} - a_p \min(P_i, 1),
\]
where $\Phi(z) = \tfrac12\bigl(1 + \operatorname{erf}(z/\sqrt2)\bigr)$ is the
normal distribution function, so $\Phi(z_i)$ is uniform on $[0,1]$ and a node is
active with probability $p_i$, but neighbouring nodes and successive stages
share their coins through $z$. Here $n^{\mathrm{on}}_i$ of its $n_i$ grid
neighbours (up to four, the row below always counting) were active in the
previous stage and $P_i$ is the contact pressure it felt in the last
relaxation (below). Growth therefore comes in patches that spread and fade,
and crowded tissue slows down.

\paragraph{Growth increment.} An active node grows by $\gamma$
(\texttt{growth\_rate}, 0.10 to 0.16 for peony, times the flower's vigour) and a
quiet one by $q\gamma$. The claw next to the receptacle grows less, and margins grow
more:
\[
\begin{gathered}
  \delta_i = \gamma\,\bigl(1 \text{ or } q\bigr)\,\min\!\Bigl(\frac{v}{3}, 1\Bigr)\bigl(1 + b_e\,m(u,v)\bigr),
  \\
  m(u,v) = \max\!\Bigl(0,\; 1 - \tfrac12\min\bigl(\min(u, \NU-1-u),\; \NV-1-v\bigr)\Bigr),
\end{gathered}
\]
so $m$ is 1 on the side edges and tip and fades to 0 two nodes in. The
increments are then smoothed by three passes that replace each node's value by
the mean of itself and its grid neighbours, as growth signals diffuse through
tissue, so folds come out as smooth bulges rather than node-sized kinks.
Finally the increment is split between length and width by the plant's
anisotropy $a$ (\texttt{growth\_anisotropy}, $-0.25$ to $0.15$):
\[
  A^\parallel_i \leftarrow A^\parallel_i\bigl(1 + \delta_i(1+a)\bigr),
  \qquad
  A^\perp_i \leftarrow A^\perp_i\bigl(1 + \delta_i(1-a)\bigr).
\]
A positive $a$ lengthens petals faster than it widens them, so they buckle
into waves across the petal; a negative $a$ widens them, so they pleat in folds
fanning from base to tip.

\paragraph{Pressure.} The pressure on a node is the sum of the contact forces
$k_{\mathrm{contact}}(d_{\min}-d)$ it takes part in (shared among a triangle's
corners by their barycentric weights) plus its envelope force
$k_{\mathrm{env}}[\rt(\x_i) - R + t_i/2]_+$. It is scaled so that $P_i = 1$
corresponds to a contact force of $k_{\mathrm{contact}}\,\tmax/4$.

\subsection{Stages and relaxation}\label{sec:stages}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Symmetry breaking.} Every unpinned point is nudged along the
  shell normal $\nabla\rt/\lVert\nabla\rt\rVert$ by
  $0.1\,w\,\tfrac{v_i}{\NV - 1}\,\xi_i$ with $\xi_i\sim\Norm(0,1)$ from stream 2
  of the flower's seed, growing from zero at the base, so buckling picks a
  direction. The envelope starts at $R = R_{\mathrm{rest}} = R_{\mathrm{env}}$,
  and the bud is relaxed once.
\item \textbf{Each stage} $g = 1,\dots,G$: compute every node's pressure; step
  each petal's growth automaton; set every rest length from the new growth;
  set the sepals' rest radius to
  $R_{\mathrm{rest}} = R_{\mathrm{env}}\bigl(1 + g_s\,g/G\bigr)$; relax.
\item \textbf{Settle.} After the last stage, relax for longer.
\end{enumerate}
Defaults: 30 relaxation iterations per stage and 75 to settle. Twice that
leaves the bud about 15\% lower in energy with the same overlaps, at twice the
cost. The fast preview preset uses 12 and 30 (not canonical,
\cref{sec:output}); tests use 15 and 15.

\paragraph{Coarse to fine.} The first $\lfloor 0.7\,G\rfloor$ stages only set
the bud's broad shape, so they are relaxed on every other control point (a
$6\times10$ grid per petal instead of $11\times19$), about four times cheaper per
iteration. The first relaxation (symmetry breaking) already runs on it.
Springs and bends join neighbours on that grid, with rest lengths from the same
laid-out sheet and growth read at their end nodes; both bending stiffnesses
(skip springs and second differences) are divided by 4, since a spring twice as long resists the
same curvature four times as hard. The growth automaton still runs on the full
grid: each node's pressure is interpolated bilinearly from the coarse grid.
Then positions are interpolated back onto the full grid (coarse points are
copied, edge midpoints take the mean of their two coarse neighbours and cell
centres the mean of four; the pinned base row keeps its own), the bud is relaxed once more, and the remaining stages, where
the petals crumple, run at full resolution. A fraction of 0 restores full
resolution throughout (\texttt{PackSettings::coarse\_fraction}). Recorded
frames from the coarse stages are interpolated the same way, and the report's
starting energy is measured on the coarse grid.

\paragraph{Relaxation by FIRE.} The energy is minimised by FIRE (Fast Inertial
Relaxation Engine), gradient descent with inertia that is reset whenever it
starts climbing. The envelope radius $R$ is one more coordinate with its own
velocity, included in the power and norms.

\paragraph{Per-node masses.} Stiff and soft parts of the bud (a thick base in
contact, a free thin edge) would otherwise need very different step sizes.
Each point gets a mass from the stiffness acting on it,
\[
  m_i = \max\bigl(d_i/200,\; 10^{-3}\bigr),
\]
where $d_i$ sums the stiffness of every spring at the point, $\zeta k$ at each
end of a bend and $4\zeta k$ at its middle, $k_{\mathrm{contact}}$ for every
candidate pair it is the point of and $k_{\mathrm{contact}}/3$ for every pair
whose triangle it is a corner of, and $k_{\mathrm{env}}$ or $k_{\mathrm{band}}$
when it is within reach of the envelope, the core or its band edge. The
envelope's mass sums $k_s$, $k_{\mathrm{env}}$ for each point near it and
$k_{\mathrm{band}}\lambda_k^2$ for each point near its band edge. FIRE runs in
mass-scaled coordinates $\vect y = \sqrt m\,\x$, and the masses are recomputed
at the start of each relaxation and after every contact-list rebuild.

With velocities $\vect v$ and scaled force $\vect f = -\nabla E/\sqrt m$ (pinned
points have zero gradient and never move), each iteration:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item computes $\nabla E$ and the power $P = \vect f\cdot \vect v$;
\item if $P > 0$, steers the velocity toward the downhill direction,
  $\vect v \leftarrow (1-\alpha)\vect v + \alpha\,\lVert\vect v\rVert\,\vect f/\lVert\vect f\rVert$,
  and after more than 5 such steps in a row sets
  $\Delta t \leftarrow \min(1.1\,\Delta t, \Delta t_{\max})$ and $\alpha \leftarrow 0.99\,\alpha$;
\item if $P \le 0$, stops: $\vect v \leftarrow 0$, $\Delta t \leftarrow \Delta t/2$, $\alpha\leftarrow 0.1$;
\item integrates $\vect v \leftarrow \vect v + \Delta t\,\vect f$ and moves each
  point by $\Delta t\,\vect v/\sqrt{m_i}$ (and $R$ likewise with its own mass),
  with each point's step clipped to length at most $\tmax/4$, and $R$'s to
  $\tmax/4$, so no petal can jump through another.
\end{enumerate}
Each relaxation starts from zero velocity, $\Delta t = \Delta t_{\max}/2$ with
$\Delta t_{\max} = 0.3$ and $\alpha = 0.1$, and runs a fixed number of
iterations; there is no convergence test, which keeps the run identical on
every platform.

\subsection{Finding contacts cheaply}
Testing every point against every triangle would be quadratic in the number of
petals. Instead a list of candidate (point, triangle) pairs closer than
\[
  \text{reach} = \tmax + \text{skin}, \qquad \text{skin} = 2\tmax,
\]
is built with a flat uniform grid (points counting-sorted into cells of size
$\max(\text{reach}, \text{extent}/64)$, enlarged by half while there are more
than $4n+64$ cells, and each triangle's padded bounding box scanned row by
row), and reused until some point has moved more than
half the skin since the list was built; it is also rebuilt at the start of
every stage. Contacts only matter below $d_{\min}\le\tmax$, and a point and a
triangle that each moved less than half the skin cannot have closed more than
one skin, so no contact is missed between rebuilds. The grid only decides
which pairs get the exact distance test; the list is sorted before use, so
results never depend on it (\cref{sec:determinism}).

Most candidates are not touching: in a packed bud only about 1\% are. Each
pair keeps its distance $d_0$ from when the list was built. Point-triangle
distance changes by at most how far the point and the triangle's corners have
moved, so if
\[
  d_0 - |\Delta\x_{\text{point}}| - \max_{\text{corners}} |\Delta\x| \ge d_{\min},
\]
the pair cannot be in contact and is skipped without the closest-point test.
This only skips pairs that the test would discard, so the result is unchanged.

\subsection{What development reports}
The core reports the energy terms before and after, the number of stages,
relaxation iterations and contact-list rebuilds, the worst overlap between two
petals $\max(d_{\min} - d)/\tmax$, the worst excursion outside a layer's band
$\max\epsilon_i/\tmax$, the envelope radius at the start and end (how much the
bud swelled), and the mean and largest accumulated growth $S_i$. Its
iteration and rebuild counts cover only the full-grid stages. These go into
the flower JSON's \texttt{meta} block. With frame recording on, the geometry and
envelope radius after every stage are kept, so the viewer can play the bud
swelling (\cref{sec:output}).

\subsection{Why it was reworked}\label{sec:packing-limits}
The first version grew every petal uniformly by one growth factor and held
each point inside its own layer's slot. Its buds read as neat nested layers
with gentle buckling, and the inner petals stopped partway up. Real peony buds
swell like marshmallows and pack in complex folds, so the rework added
per-node growth from the automaton, a sepal envelope that gives way, a loose
band so petals can cross layers, and a finer $11\times19$ grid that can hold
small crumples. Later changes made growth correlated, persistent and
directional, added the zigzag-resistant bending term against grid-scale
crinkle, preconditioned FIRE with per-node masses, and moved the early stages
to a coarse grid.
